# Analysis guide

How to make each core part of the prompt specific. The skeleton holds the default checklists; this file says what good looks like. Examples are illustrative.

## 0A and 0B

- Weak: "Why now: strong growth momentum." Strong: "Why now: the 400 TPD plant was commissioned in Aug 2026 [Q1 FY27 call, CEO]; Q2 is its first full quarter."
- The core debate is one question the evidence can settle: "Can the new plant run above 70% utilisation by FY28 without a price war?"
- "What the market seems to believe" uses arithmetic: market cap / trailing PAT = 45x, so the price assumes about 25% PAT growth for 5 years [E, working shown].
- Each of the 15 questions names a number, customer, plant or person. "What is the moat?" is weak; "Why did BHEL place a ₹1,200 Cr order in Mar 2026, and has it ordered before?" is strong.

## Business (S2)

- Everyday picture, then the revenue equation, then ₹100 of sales.
- Judge model quality plainly: one-time plant sales with no service tail is weaker than recurring supply; say so.
- Every contract term gets a line on who benefits and when it bites.

## Products (S3)

- Build the type grid from the website product list and the deck.
- NOT DISCLOSED is a valid cell.
- What's new separates firm orders from "likely" or "in discussion".

## Customers and moat (S5, S6)

- Named customers only from filings, decks or calls; if none are named, say so.
- Each moat claim gets a counter-point: who else can do it, and how long it would take them.

## Capacity and capex (S9)

- Existing, live now, planned. Utilisation from management, or derived [E].
- One row per project; every date and cost tracked from first mention to now.
- Return on past capex: incremental revenue / incremental gross block, before vs after.

## Management (S10, S11)

- Pledges checked in primary filings (shareholding pledge column, SAST disclosures): Verified or Not found.
- Every raise traced to its use against the stated objects.
- Tone per call with the five labels; hard analyst questions quoted verbatim with the answer.
- Credibility = promises met / promises due, as a %.

## Guidance (S12)

- Verbatim quotes with speaker and date. No number given means "No number given", never an inferred one.
- What must be true: the H2 growth needed for the full-year guide, worked out.

## Growth and triggers (S13)

- Each driver sized bottom-up and capped by capacity and balance sheet.
- STRONG needs 2 or more primary filings with numbers and a date.
- Test every trigger the user expects; if absent, say so in its own row.

## Anti-thesis (S19)

- Argue as a short seller would, with numbers: receivable days, cash conversion, concentration, return on capex.
- Risk cards carry a threshold signal, such as "debtor days above 150 for two quarters".
- The bear case in section 5 uses these cards.

## Valuation (section 5)

- Metric from the `business-valuation-scenarios` Step 1 table.
- Scenario build and layout follow the `business-valuation-scenarios` skill, Steps 2 to 4 (read it there): base equals guidance exactly; bull is guidance realised faster or better from disclosed triggers; bear misses guidance for named risks; three separate year-wise tables with two historical years, then the caveats.
- Diluted share count always.
