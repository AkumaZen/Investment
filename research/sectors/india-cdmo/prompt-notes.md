## Diagnosis before drafting

1. Core debate: which suppliers can convert evidenced outsourcing demand into qualified work, repeat revenue and cash returns, and what does the price already require?
2. Unit economics: research bills time or work packages; development bills defined projects/milestones; manufacturing bills released saleable units/batches and related services. These are different contracts. Qualification, yield, batch timing and capital constrain sales.
3. Management: compare controllers, incentives, capex/M&A decisions and due promises issuer by issuer. No sector-wide management-quality claim is established at prompt stage.
4. Guidance and triggers: retrieve the latest original guidance and all historical revisions. Track commissioning, validation, first commercial supply, ramp and cash return as separate milestones. Never turn a capability announcement into a revenue forecast.
5. Anti-thesis: sponsor funding and clinical failures can reduce work while simultaneous supplier capex creates idle assets. Even good demand can produce weak cash returns or be overpriced. Customer concentration and regulation can overwhelm an attractive TAM.
6. Playbook: services plus process manufacturing, a heterogeneous outsourcing cycle, capital ramps and corporate-action comparability. Global peers are demand/capability benchmarks; Indian-listed businesses are the investment universe.
7. Main distinction: innovator CDMO, generic/formulation contractors, research services and adjacent merchant APIs have different buyers and economics. The first unproven link is often sponsor interest becoming a qualified, executable supplier order.
8. Questions: 15 sector-specific questions in 0B, involving named issuers and explicit periods/stress assumptions. Stress numbers are labelled assumptions, not discoveries.
9. Connecting the dots: issuer identity prevents Suven/Cohance double counting; source-grounded global programme/backlog facts illustrate why programme count and headline backlog need separate conversion analysis. A second hypothetical utilisation example exposes EBITDA versus capital-return differences.
10. Conflicts: issuer genealogy, acquired versus organic history, consolidated versus CDMO revenue, service/modality market definitions, drug substance/product overlap, installed/qualified capacity, backlog/milestones, FX/fiscal years, and unavailable segment capital.
11. Valuation: metric follows each cohort and company. Use the installed business-valuation-scenarios Steps 1-4; add capital/FCF and reverse-expectations checks. SOTP is useful only with defensible segment economics. rNPV belongs only to owned drug rights.
12. Structure: retain all 28 core topics and S1-S20 sequence. Rename S8 to demand cycle/global competition and S9 to qualified capacity; extend S11 historical execution and S14 issuer atlas/cohort comparability. Add no arbitrary page quota; detailed valuation and verdict stay in S20. Exclude routine compliance and unrelated chemical/pharma market totals with reasons.
13. Visual decisions: use the five-question method and 16 question/insight/form/source rows. AX is the sector-comparison family; physical page precedents and measured versus chosen typography are recorded in evidence/design-notes.md. dataviz was not found; the installed framework provides the fallback. Complementary installed research skills and conflicts are recorded in external-skills.md.
