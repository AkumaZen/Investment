# India CDMO demand and opportunity evidence

**As-of date:** 10 November 2026. This is a primary-source evidence memo for prompt design, not an investable market forecast or issuer report. Company statements are attributed as such; company views are not independent market proof. No single market-size estimate is treated as definitive.

## Source-anchored findings and tensions

1. **Biologics outsourcing demand is structurally supported, but commercial scale matters more than early pipeline counts.** Lonza’s FY2025 Annual Report says biologics pipeline grew about 9% annually over the prior decade, antibodies were about half of biopharma market, and mammalian plus drug-product CDMO markets were expected to grow low-to-mid teens over 2025–30. It says large pharma provides most CDMO revenue through commercial manufacturing, while small biotech is a larger share of pipeline; biotech funding volatility has limited direct impact on Lonza because early-stage is about 10% of its CDMO business. These are Lonza-cited estimates/observations, not India-specific addressable spend. [Lonza FY2025 Integrated Biologics](https://www.lonza.com/annualreport/2025/pages/integrated-biologics)

2. **Capacity is already ample at global scale in aggregate, while utilization and customer fit determine who captures spend.** Lonza’s FY2025 investor presentation said mammalian CDMO capacity had risen above 50% of global capacity and pharma capital spending likely shifts toward the US; Lonza separately reported a 330,000 L Vacaville footprint and is ramping 20,000 L Visp capacity. At the same time, Samsung Biologics’ Q3 2026 release said Plants 1–4 operated at full capacity, Plant 5 was ramping, and cumulative contract orders exceeded US$21.9bn, with 20% FY2026 revenue growth guidance. The mixed evidence cautions against equating pipeline growth with uniform greenfield need or assuming Indian suppliers automatically capture unmet demand. [Lonza FY2025 results presentation](https://www.lonza.com/-/media/Lonza/Lonzacom/investor-relations/Financial%20Presentations/29012026_full_year_presentation), [Lonza FY2025 Integrated Biologics](https://www.lonza.com/annualreport/2025/pages/integrated-biologics), [Samsung Biologics Q3 2026 results](https://samsungbiologics.com/media/company-news/samsung-biologics-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results)

3. **Small molecules remain the clearest large-scale Indian capability pool; the global incumbent evidence shows molecule progression and commercial conversion are the value driver.** WuXi AppTec reported FY2025 Chemistry revenue RMB 36.47bn (+25.5%), D&M revenue RMB 19.92bn (+11.4%), 3,452 small-molecule D&M pipeline molecules, including 83 commercial and 91 Phase III; 310 molecules converted from discovery to development. Reactor volume exceeded 4,000 kL across API sites. These metrics demonstrate an incumbent benchmark, not spend automatically available to Indian suppliers. [WuXi AppTec FY2025 results](https://www.wuxiapptec.com/news/news/wuxi-apptec-beat-full-year-guidance-and-achieved-record-performance-in-2025)

4. **Peptides/TIDES are a distinct, presently capacity-constrained opportunity, but high-profile GLP-1 demand should not be generalized to every peptide or every Indian plant.** WuXi AppTec said TIDES (peptides and oligonucleotides grouped together) revenue rose 96% in FY2025 to RMB 11.37bn, backlog rose 20.2%, and TIDES D&M customer/molecule counts grew 25%/45%. Its report does not disaggregate peptide from oligo demand, commercial volumes, or backlog executable timing. [WuXi AppTec FY2025 results](https://www.wuxiapptec.com/news/news/wuxi-apptec-beat-full-year-guidance-and-achieved-record-performance-in-2025)

5. **Bachem’s report supports a real peptide scale-up signal and simultaneously highlights execution dependence.** Bachem’s FY2025 report describes unusually strong peptide demand, with demand exceeding capacity, and says first-phase Building K commercial production ramps gradually in 2026; successful ramp-up is a prerequisite for its 2026 sales target. In its 2025 half-year report, Bachem described peptide demand moving from kg to ton-scale, particularly for diabetes/obesity and expanded indications. These are supplier observations; they do not quantify third-party addressable tonnage for India. [Bachem FY2025 Annual Report](https://www.bachem.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Bachem-Annual-report-2025-EN.pdf), [Bachem HY2025 report](https://www.bachem.com/download/455/hyr/251201/e_halbjahresbericht_2025_web)

6. **Biologics orders can be long dated and milestone-heavy; backlog is not revenue.** WuXi Biologics disclosed US$23.7bn total backlog at 31 Dec 2025, consisting of US$11.5bn service backlog and US$12.2bn potential milestones; only US$4.5bn was within three years. The company attributes growth to utilization and operations, while its total includes conditional milestones. Prompt analyses should split committed service work from potential milestone value, timing, termination rights and cancellation history. [WuXi Biologics FY2025 results](https://www.wuxibiologics.com/press-release/wuxi-biologics-reports-record-2025-annual-results/)

7. **ADC activity creates integrated demand, but ADC capability is a chain of separate qualification gates.** FDA’s 2025 novel-drug list includes Datroway (datopotamab deruxtecan), an ADC, approved 17 Jan 2025; Lonza says ADC drug sales are projected above 20% CAGR over 2025–30 and customers increasingly seek full-chain providers. The ADC workbench spans payload/linker chemistry, conjugation, biologic drug substance, potent containment, analytics and sterile fill-finish; evidence for one capability does not establish qualification for all. [FDA 2025 novel approvals](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2025), [Lonza FY2025 Integrated Biologics](https://www.lonza.com/annualreport/2025/pages/integrated-biologics)

8. **India sector totals are not comparable without scope normalization.** CRISIL Ratings’ May 2025 note defines its CRDMO population as 55% CDMO (development/clinical/commercial manufacturing) and 45% CRO (research/discovery), estimates ₹70,000 crore FY2025 revenue across 19 rated firms representing ~50% of sector, and says India is under 5% of global CRDMO. The BCG/India report describes Indian CRDMO at US$3–3.5bn today and US$22–25bn by 2035. These scopes/time bases differ from global CDMO-only estimates and from broad pharmaceutical manufacturing; do not combine them. [CRISIL Ratings](https://www.crisilratings.com/en/home/newsroom/press-releases/2025/05/pharma-crdmo-revenue-to-grow-13-15percent-amid-supply-chain-derisking.html), [BCG India CRDMO report page](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-unleashing-the-tiger-indian-crdmo-sector)

9. **India’s real qualification gap is modality- and site-specific.** India’s FDA-inspected facility status is verifiable through FDA’s inspection database, but an inspection classification attaches to a facility/inspection and product scope, not to national or company-wide modality readiness. For each Indian site, establish the exact dosage form/modality, cGMP scope, inspection outcome/date, commercial product track record, scale, available capacity, analytical capability, and customer audits/validation. [FDA Inspection and Enforcement Resources](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/pharmaceutical-quality-resources/inspection-enforcement-resources). As a concrete example of a company self-report, Syngene’s FY2025 annual report says it is expanding monoclonal-antibody production capacity; expansion does not by itself establish commercial approvals or utilization. [Syngene FY2025 CDMO report](https://annualreport.syngeneintl.com/contract-development-and-manufacturing-services.html)

10. **Policy-driven China diversification is an opportunity catalyst, not proof of automatic Indian share transfer.** The FY2026 US NDAA was enacted 18 Dec 2025 and includes BIOSECURE Act §851. As summarized in a current SEC filing, the law restricts US agencies from procuring/contracting for biotechnology equipment/services from designated biotechnology companies of concern, and limits federal loan/grant spending; OMB must publish a formal BCC list by Dec 2026, after which FAR revision and a 60-day effective period follow. The same filing reports WuXi AppTec’s 1260H designation was preliminarily enjoined 7 Aug 2026 during litigation; final scope/status remains uncertain. The enacted framework is narrower and process-dependent versus the 2024 proposed bill; private-sector supply decisions may still go beyond legal minimums, but that is a hypothesis to verify customer by customer. [SEC filing, filed Nov 2026, BIOSECURE disclosure](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1812364/000119312526447294/rlay-20260930.htm), [enacted federal statutory text, 50 USC §4565a and NDAA §851](https://uscode.house.gov/)

11. **Demand cycles depend on sponsor capital and approved-product volumes, not only pipeline growth.** Lonza says biotech funding became more selective and concentrated in larger/later rounds in 2025, while its own early-stage utilization remained high. CRISIL says Indian CRO growth depends on venture funding and cautions recovery may be delayed; commercial CDMO growth instead rests on orders, enquiries and manufacturing conversions. This supports separate analysis of clinical-stage customer mix versus commercial demand. [Lonza FY2025 Integrated Biologics](https://www.lonza.com/annualreport/2025/pages/integrated-biologics), [CRISIL Ratings](https://www.crisilratings.com/en/home/newsroom/press-releases/2025/05/pharma-crdmo-revenue-to-grow-13-15percent-amid-supply-chain-derisking.html)

12. **Large sponsor commitments can materially alter near-term demand visibility, but deal-level disclosures should be tested for volume and qualification status.** Siegfried’s 2026 update says its earlier product-level guidance awaited confirmation of volumes for a large contract; those volumes were then included in revised group guidance. The contrast illustrates why “strategic partnership,” inbound inquiries, pipeline and announced capex are weaker evidence than named/qualified programs, confirmed commercial volumes and disclosed revenue contribution. [Siegfried 2026 update](https://www.siegfried.ch/siegfried-delivers-profitable-growth-and-confirms-outlook/)

## Definitions and pitfalls the sector prompt must enforce

- **TAM vs. outsourced market vs. India-addressable revenue:** TAM may mean drug sales, total manufacturing spend, all externalized R&D/manufacturing, or only third-party CDMO fees. Exclude sponsor in-house production and distinguish CRO, generic contract manufacturing, innovator CDMO, API, formulation, fill-finish, and packaging.
- **Gross market vs. serviceable market:** Apply molecule type, process route, GMP stage, site qualification, scale, geography, client audits, existing capacity, input availability and price economics before assigning a supplier addressable pool.
- **Modality boundaries:** “Biologics” can include mAbs, recombinant proteins, vaccines, cell/gene products and drug product; ADCs require biologic, payload/linker, conjugation and sometimes fill-finish; peptide and oligonucleotide TIDES aggregates are not interchangeable. Small-molecule API capability is not peptide/oligo qualification.
- **Backlog:** Separate firm service orders, cancellable/reschedulable orders, minimum purchase obligations, options, milestones contingent on clinical/regulatory events and maximum contract value. Report expected execution period and cancellation/deferral evidence. Do not divide all backlog by one year’s revenue and call it visibility.
- **Pipeline:** Count molecules by phase and probability of progressing; identify duplicate assets across trials, indications, formulations and manufacturers. A molecule entering a CDMO pipeline is not an order, commercial product or volume commitment.
- **Revenue and growth:** Normalize currency, organic vs acquired growth, pass-through materials, one-time technology transfer, inventory destocking/restocking, utilization, price/mix and capacity ramp. Separate reported sales from management “market” estimates.
- **Qualification:** Distinguish FDA registration/listing, inspection, inspection classification, product-specific approval/PLI, client audit, technology transfer completion, PPQ batches, and commercial launch. None alone proves available capacity or customer demand.
- **Catalent ownership:** Catalent was acquired by Novo Holdings and ceased being an independent public listed issuer in 2024; treat it as a private Novo Holdings portfolio operating company within Novo Nordisk’s transaction structure, not as a current public peer with independent listed filings. See Novo Holdings’ [acquisition completion notice](https://novoholdings.dk/news/novo-holdings-completes-acquisition-of-catalent/) and Novo Nordisk’s [transaction announcement](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=166367).
- **Funnel and timing:** Clinical pipeline → CDMO selection → tech transfer → GMP/PPQ → approval → demand ramp; many years and attrition separate early pipeline from commercial production. Keep stage-based demand distinct.

## Sector-specific research questions (15)

1. What is the India CDMO market definition used by each source: CRO+CDMO, drug-substance only, formulation/fill-finish, generic contract manufacturing, or innovator CRDMO? Which figures can be reconciled to a common scope and currency year?
2. For each Indian supplier/site, which small-molecule API/intermediate/product classes have current FDA/EMA/PMDA inspection evidence, commercial supply history, and free validated capacity? Which are merely planned or under qualification?
3. How much reported small-molecule revenue is custom innovator work versus generic APIs, intermediates, captive production, and pass-through materials? How do price, duration and margin differ?
4. Which sponsor programs are backed by confirmed commercial volumes or minimum purchase commitments, and what are the start dates, ramp assumptions, termination rights and renewal terms?
5. For biologics, what is installed and validated mammalian capacity by Indian site (L), molecule/process, disposable vs stainless configuration, downstream trains, fill-finish, current utilization and available slots? Which capacities have completed PPQ or commercial release?
6. Does the Indian provider have end-to-end mAb capability, or only upstream/downstream, analytical, development, biosimilar, vaccine, or fill-finish services? What prevents transfer from clinical to commercial scale?
7. For ADCs, which chain links are actually offered in-country: potent payload/linker synthesis, conjugation, biologic drug substance, containment, analytics, sterile fill-finish? What audited customer programs and clinical/commercial batches evidence each link?
8. For peptides, separate synthetic route (SPPS/LPPS), peptide length/modifications, annual GMP tonnage, purification/lyophilization bottlenecks, impurity standards, customer commitments, and commercial launch volumes. How much demand is GLP-1 versus other therapeutic areas?
9. For oligonucleotides, what sequence length/chemistry, synthesis scale, purification, conjugation, analytical release and GMP capacity is installed and qualified? Are reported TIDES orders peptide, oligo or bundled?
10. Which approved drugs and late-stage trials will generate new manufacturing volumes over the next 1–5 years? Use FDA/EMA labels and trial registries; deduplicate molecule/indication/site and probability-weight phase transitions.
11. What do global CDMO counterparties report on utilization, pipeline conversion, cancellations, price concessions and customer-funded capex? Does their experience contradict a generalized capacity shortage?
12. How have biotech funding, IPOs, follow-on offerings, venture rounds and sponsor R&D budgets changed by stage and geography, and how does that map to Indian CRO/early-phase CDMO enquiries versus commercial orders?
13. How much business has explicitly moved from China to India due to procurement rules, customer risk policy or dual-sourcing? Distinguish named wins and transferred volumes from management expectations and broad “China+1” commentary.
14. What is the enacted BIOSECURE Act’s current effective scope, designated entity list and timeline, and what private-sector action is occurring beyond federal-contract rules? Track litigation and implementation; avoid treating proposed bill language as operative law.
15. What capex is committed, spent and commissioned by modality, and what orderbook/utilization is required for returns? Test cancellations, customer concentration, input dependencies (resins, amino acids, solvents, media, single-use systems), hiring, regulatory readiness and ramp delays in downside cases.

## Uncertainty register

- Most market CAGR/TAM numbers are consultancy or vendor estimates with inconsistent scope; this memo uses only to illustrate the scope problem, not as a forecast.
- Public issuer reports often aggregate pipeline, backlog and revenue across modalities; disclosure rarely reveals exact molecule-level outsourced spend, contracted volumes or cancellation rates.
- India site qualification and commercial capacity evidence is incomplete at sector level. FDA inspection status cannot be used as a blanket proxy for modality, customer, process or usable capacity.
- BIOSECURE implementation and litigation were still evolving as of 10 Nov 2026; statutory text and dated SEC disclosure should be rechecked immediately before any company research.
- I found no suitable primary-source industry-wide order cancellation series or sponsor-level outsourced budget dataset in this pass. Treat these as explicit research gaps.
