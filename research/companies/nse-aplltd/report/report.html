Alembic Pharmaceuticals Ltd

Q2 FY27 step-up: structural or one-off?

Quick analysis, not a recommendation  |  Data as of 10 November 2026  |  Rs crore unless stated

Summary

A real step-up in the base, a temporary boost in the margin

The base business has moved up a gear, but most of the record Q2 margin comes from a launch whose exclusivity ends this month.

Alembic's revenue jumped 36% in Q2 FY27 and its operating margin hit 22%. For five years before that, sales grew about 8.5% a year and the margin sat between 12% and 16%. This report answers one question: is that jump the new normal, or a one-time boost?

The short answer

  • Structural, a little over half of the growth. Strip out the launch block and Q2 revenue still grew about 20%. Management says the US base portfolio grew 23% without new launches, and the US base now runs at about $75m a quarter, against $60m to $65m before.
  • One-off, most of the margin. About Rs 307 cr of Q2 US sales sits above the base path [E]. We estimate it earned about Rs 188 to 210 cr of EBITDA, roughly a third of the quarter's total. Without it, the Q2 margin is about 16.5%, no better than a year ago.
  • The key launch, bosutinib, loses its 180-day exclusivity in late November 2026. Management itself says it does not know what happens after that.
  • What can make the margin step-up stick: the US branded business stops losing money in FY28 (worth 150 to 200 bp), the new plants fill up, and first-to-file launches such as dapagliflozin and binimetinib arrive. These are disclosed plans, not results yet.

Our working view: a sustainable margin of about 18% to 20%, not 22%. The price of Rs 1,017 is 22x the earnings per share our base case expects for FY27, a year that includes the exclusivity boost. So the stock already pays for guidance being met.

What happened

One segment, the US, did most of the work

Q2 FY27 revenue was Rs 2,603 cr, up 36%, and the US alone added Rs 437 cr of the Rs 693 cr increase.

  • US sales rose 77% in Q2 (72% for US generics alone), helped by the bosutinib launch in June 2026 and a weaker rupee.
  • API (bulk drug sales to other drug makers) grew 39%. Management guides at least high-teens growth for the year, so part of this is lumpy order timing.
  • India grew 14% in Q2: human health 11%, animal health 23%. That is the best India quarter in this set, after 5% to 7% for most of FY26.
  • Ex-US markets (Europe, Canada, Australia and others) grew 12%, in line with their mid-teens trend.

What this means for you

The quarter's strength is concentrated in the US. Any view on Alembic now rests on what the US looks like once the launch boost fades.

The core question

The growth is mostly real; the margin is mostly the launch

About 56% of the Q2 sales increase came from the base business, but about 34% of Q2 EBITDA came from the launch block [E].

"If you take the new launches out, so the base portfolio grew by 23% in the quarter."

Management, Q2 FY27 call, p6

Limits of this split: the company does not disclose bosutinib revenue, and the 23% base growth includes some currency gain. The launch block also holds a few smaller H1 launches. Treat the split as a range, not a reported number.

What this means for you

Do not extrapolate the 22% margin. The part that looks structural is base growth in the high teens to low twenties and a margin that can reach 18% to 20% if the branded loss ends on schedule.

Context

A steady, slow grower that has just spent heavily on new plants

Before FY27, Alembic grew sales about 8.5% a year with a 12% to 16% margin; the new plants built in that period are what FY27 is starting to use.

  • R&D fell from 15.8% of sales in FY22 to 7.6% in FY24 and was 9.6% in FY26; US filings fell from 23 in FY22 to 8 in FY25 and 10 in FY26. In our view, the lower filing years limit launches from FY29 onward.
  • Cumulative: 283 US filings, 245 approvals and 188 products launched [Q2 deck].
  • Promoters own 69.87%, steady, with no pledge shown on Screener.

What this means for you

History argues for caution on any long-term margin above 20%. The new element is capacity that was idle and is now filling.

What is coming

The pipeline offers more exclusivity windows, each one temporary

Two of the four announcements add lasting capacity use (Olopatadine, DifGen); two are first-to-file chances that, like bosutinib, would be large but temporary.

  • Management guides more than 30 US launches over 18 months, about a quarter first-to-file or shared day-1 [Q2 call p4, p12].
  • A steady flow of exclusivity windows can make one-off gains recur. That is the bull case. It is not yet a track record: bosutinib is the first large one in this set of documents.
  • Dapagliflozin's launch date depends on its patent position, which the documents do not disclose.

What this means for you

Count each exclusivity launch as a one-off when it lands. If they arrive every year or two, the average lifts; watch the launch count each quarter against the 30-in-18-months target.

Guidance

Guidance was raised twice in six months, and still implies a weaker second half

The 20% to 25% FY27 guide implies H2 sales of Rs 4,061 to 4,428 cr, 7% to 15% below H1, but still 9% to 19% above H2 FY26.

"guidance incorporates the expected normalization of exclusivity-led sales"

Management, Q2 FY27 call, p5

What this means for you

Management is telling you the exclusivity boost fades in H2. A Q3 that falls back toward Rs 2,000 to 2,200 cr is in line with guidance, not a miss.

Credibility

Good on the US, slow on India

Management met or beat its US and group targets in this period, but India took longer than promised and the branded loss got bigger before it shrinks.

"historically, Alembic has not been a high-growth company."

Shaunak Amin, Q3 FY26 call, p11

The one number management got clearly wrong was the size of bosutinib, and it was too low. That cuts both ways: it shows how much one exclusivity launch can move the numbers, and how much can fall away.

What this means for you

Give weight to the US guidance, which has been met. Give less weight to India and margin promises until two more quarters confirm them.

Monitoring

Q3 FY27 is the first real test

The next two quarters will show whether the base holds above 20% growth once bosutinib exclusivity ends in late November 2026.

Risks

  • R1, bosutinib erodes faster. Q1 call p8: erosion will be 'quite drastic' because volumes are small. If H2 sales come in at the bottom of the guide, FY27 PAT growth drops sharply in FY28.
  • R2, the rupee reverses. About 11 to 12 points of Q1 US growth was currency. A stronger rupee removes it.
  • R3, US price erosion. FY23's margin of 12% shows how fast US pricing can hit Alembic.
  • R4, India keeps lagging. India has grown about 6% a year for four years; the new head of human health is still new.
  • R5, cash. FY25 cash from operations was Rs 88 cr on Rs 1,008 cr EBITDA. New US launches again lengthen collection cycles.
  • Anti-thesis in one line: take out the launch block and Q2 was a 20% growth, 16% to 17% margin quarter, priced at 22x trailing earnings that include the one-off.

What this means for you

If Q3 US sales hold above $90m and margin stays at 18% or more, the structural case strengthens. If both fall back, the bear case is the better guide.

Valuation

The price already pays for guidance being met

At 22x earnings, FY29 value is about Rs 940 in the bear case, Rs 1,399 in the base case and Rs 1,689 in the bull case, against Rs 1,017 today.

Why P/E: Alembic is profitable and its earnings are not in a deep cycle, so earnings per share is the most direct measure. EV/EBITDA is shown as a check because it includes the Rs 1,236 cr of net debt.

How to read the year-by-year tables. Each case is laid out like a Screener profit and loss page: two actual years, then our three projected years. The target price at 31 March of each year is that year's earnings per share times 22, not discounted. Return and CAGR are measured from Rs 1,017 on 10 November 2026. FY27E CAGR is annualised over the 0.39 years to 31 Mar 2027, so small moves show as large yearly rates; read it with the return row. No warrants or convertibles were found in the documents read, so earnings per share is the same before and after dilution.

Key assumptions and caveats

  • Guided by management: FY27 revenue growth about 20% to 25% including the expected normalisation of exclusivity sales (Q2 FY27 call p5); FY28 US growth about 10% to 12% (p13); EBITDA margin 'closer to 20%' for FY28 to FY29 (p14); tax 25% from FY27 (p5); no branded drag in FY28 (p11).
  • Estimated by us [E]: FY28 and FY29 group growth (guidance covers only the US for FY28), every bear and bull line, other income Rs 20 to 30 cr, depreciation Rs 400 to 455 cr, interest Rs 130 to 105 cr.
  • Margin basis: the model uses Screener's operating profit, which ran about Rs 16 cr a quarter below company EBITDA in FY27 (conflict C2); the FY27 base margin of 19.5% is the company-basis H1 figure (conflict C1).
  • Share count: 19.66 cr shares (market cap / price, face value Rs 2). No warrants or convertibles found in the documents read, so pre and post-dilution EPS are the same.
  • The P/E is held flat at 22x across years and scenarios. That is a simplification: a bear outcome would usually also compress the multiple, and a bull outcome could expand it.

What this means for you

The base case gives about 14% a year to March 2029 if management delivers its guidance with no slip. The bear case loses money even at an unchanged 22x, and a bear outcome would usually come with a lower multiple too. This is not investment advice.
