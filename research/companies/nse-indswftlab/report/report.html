A real step-up in sales, not yet a proven business, sitting on cash and a stake

S1 | Summary

A real step-up in sales that still has to turn into cash

The first-half jump is real, but it rests on new partner launches and has not yet produced cash; at Rs 349 the price is fair to slightly cheap if the second half delivers, and fragile if it does not.

Ind-Swift Laboratories makes tablets, capsules and sachets in Punjab and Jammu and sells most of them abroad, under its own registrations or for partner companies such as Viatris. Until 2024 it was mainly a maker of drug ingredients (APIs). It sold that business for Rs 1,650 cr, paid off its debt, kept a 7.80% stake in the buyer, and merged in its sister company's finished-medicine business.

In the first half of FY27 sales rose 35.8% to Rs 421.0 cr and operating margin jumped from 5.5% to 18.0%. This report answers one question first: is that step-up a durable export business heading to Rs 1,500 cr, or a launch bump sitting on top of cash and a stake?

Our answer: the step-up is real in the profit and loss account, but it is not yet a proven business. Most of the growth came from new partner launches: about Rs 80 to 90 cr of the Rs 111.0 cr increase was from new customers, and one partner molecule went from Rs 25 cr in Q1 to Rs 58 cr in Q2. The first link that is not yet proven is repeat orders turning into cash. Receivables and inventory rose Rs 131.8 cr in a year, and operating cash flow was negative for three years running.

At Rs 349, cash and the stake (at management's own value) make up about 32% of the market value. The rest, about Rs 2,063 cr, buys the medicine business at 11.4 times management's FY27 operating profit. That is cheap only if the second half delivers: sales of Rs 533 cr, +64% on last year's second half.

Management has delivered 2 of 22 claims we tracked, changed 7 and misstated 3, all three misstatements understating its own progress. We rate it WATCH, not RED.

S2 | The everyday picture

A medicine maker that sells abroad, mostly through other people's hands

Ind-Swift makes copies of off-patent medicines in India and sells about 83% of them abroad; it earns from approvals it holds and from partners it supplies, and it waits about 103 days to be paid.

The revenue equation

Sales = own brands (registrations launched × units × price) + contract supply (partner volume × transfer price) + India (brands × units × price, plus P2P orders).

The three parts behave differently. Own brands grow slowly as each new registration earns money about two years after it is filed. Contract supply can jump in a quarter when a partner launches, but the partner decides the volume. India is small and was shrinking in FY26.

How it makes money

Contract terms and who benefits

The value chain

1. Develop

R&D at Panchkula (70+ scientists) builds a copy of an off-patent medicine. About Rs 12 cr a year.
→

2. Register

A dossier is filed in each country. 1 to 1.5 years per approval; 900+ held, 540+ in its own name.
→

3. Approve the site

Regulators inspect the plant. Derabassi: UK MHRA, Australia TGA, Health Canada.
→

4. Make

Tablets, capsules, sachets at Derabassi (export) and Samba (India). 70-75% used.
→

5. Sell

Own brands through local distributors, or contract supply to partners like Viatris. About 83% of sales is export.
→

6. Get paid

Partners and distributors pay on credit. Receivables about 103 days of sales today.

Finished dosage form: the tablet, capsule or sachet a patient takes.

A country's approval to sell one product from one site, also called a marketing authorisation.

CMO / CDMO

The agreed price per pack a partner pays; Ind-Swift's margin is what is left after its cost.
P2P

EOU

EU / PIC/S
European and international inspection standards that open regulated markets.
EBITDA
Operating profit before depreciation, interest and tax.
Working capital
Cash tied up in stock and unpaid bills, less what is owed to suppliers.
Warrant
A right to buy a new share later at a fixed price; 25% is paid upfront.
CCD
Compulsorily convertible debenture: the instrument in which the Synthimed stake is held.
[E] and [G]
[E] is our estimate or calculation; [G] is management guidance.

What this means for you

The business is simple to describe but slow to prove: each approval takes years to earn money, and the fastest-growing part, contract supply, is the part where the partner holds the power. Watch the share of sales from own brands; the company aims for 65:35 own brands to contract supply.

What this means for you

S4 | Where the money comes from

Exports carry the business; India is small and shrinking

About 83% of sales is export, split roughly two-thirds own brands and one-third contract supply; the company does not disclose profit by line, so which line earns the 18% margin is unknown.

The call gave Q2 own-brand exports as Rs 125 cr and contract supply as Rs 65 cr; our split of the deck shares gives Rs 131 cr and Rs 63 cr. The deck puts the export split at 69:31, the call at 68:32; the target is 65:35. India fell 14.7% in FY26 while exports rose 21.3%.

Source: Q2 FY27 call transcript, Wed, 4 Nov 2026 (machine transcript supplied by the reader; BSE carries the audio), p.5; Q2 FY27 investor presentation, filed Wed, 4 Nov 2026, p.20; Annual Report FY2025-26, p.93.

What this means for you

Own brands earn a higher gross margin than contract supply (50-55% vs about 42%), so a rising contract share can lift sales while holding margin back. Ask for line-level profit; until it is given, the margin story rests on totals.

S5 | Customers and partners

A few partners drive the growth, and they set the volumes

Viatris and one European partner explain most of the new growth; both are real and supplying, but neither contract's length, minimums or price resets is disclosed.

About Rs 80 to 90 cr of the Rs 111.0 cr first-half increase came from new customers, which is 72% to 81% of the growth [E]. The partner molecule's Rs 83 cr in H1 is an upper bound on its share of the increase, because its H1 FY26 sales are not disclosed.

S6 | What protects it

Approvals and partner ties give a moderate moat, not a wide one

The advantages are regulatory approvals and partner exclusivity, which take years to copy; they are real but shared with many Indian exporters, and none yet shows in high returns on capital.

Site approvals (MHRA, TGA, Health Canada)

MODERATE

Derabassi is approved by three strict regulators; a new site takes years to qualify.

Counter-point: Approvals are company claims; regulator databases were not checked, and EU GMP status is not stated.

Registration library

MODERATE

900+ approvals, 540+ in its own name; each takes 1 to 1.5 years.

Counter-point: How many earn money is not disclosed; average yield is about Rs 0.93 cr per own registration a year [E].

Exclusive partner supply

MODERATE

Sole worldwide maker for two Viatris products; the partner filed variations naming the site.

Counter-point: The partner holds the customer and the volume; a transfer price can be renegotiated.

Low-cost Indian manufacturing

WEAK

Export-oriented unit with Indian labour costs; margin rose to 18% on a flat staff bill.

Counter-point: Every Indian generic exporter has this; it is a ticket to play, not a moat.

Customer relationships in India (P2P)

WEAK

Cipla, Lupin, Mankind and Concord as repeat clients, paid in advance.

Counter-point: Only 5% of sales; India fell in FY26.

Brand

WEAK

Ethical brands with 25,000+ doctors.

Counter-point: About 5% of sales; not a source of advantage abroad.

S10 | Management

Delivering on margin, loose on promises and on where the cash sits

Verdict WATCH: no qualified audit, no disclosed pledge and no reported deviation in use of funds, but loans without terms, a promoter-linked treasury book, steep pay rises for promoter directors and a 20% credibility score on judged claims.

Who they are

The Munjal family controls the board; the CFO is the public face on calls. Board independence and succession plans were not assessed beyond the annual report's governance section, and the deck's experience bullets do not line up cleanly with names, so we do not attribute tenures.

Skin in the game

Key managers were paid Rs 16.26 cr in FY26 (Rs 12.65 cr in FY25), about 38% of standalone profit of Rs 42.56 cr [E]. Two promoter directors got raises of 72% and 100% while the median employee got 8.07%.

Capital allocation

The company holds Rs 28.85 cr of shares in Essix Biosciences, the promoter entity that is funding it through warrants. Money flows both ways between the company and its promoter.

Do they deliver?

Of 22 claims we tracked from Jan to Nov 2026, 15 can be judged today. Three were met as stated (2 delivered, 1 on track): a credibility score of 20% of judged claims. Seven changed, two missed and three were misstated. Detail in S11.

How they communicate

Hard questions get deferrals rather than numbers. Asked how many registrations earn revenue, the CFO said "we can come back to you on this" [call p11]. Asked for FY28, he said a number would come on the next call [call p6].

Hard red flags and fairness to minorities

What this means for you

S11 | Claims checked

Targets drift up while the building blocks drift down

Management met 3 of 15 claims that can be judged (20%), changed 7, missed 2 and misstated 3; the misstatements were careless rather than flattering, but they lower trust in spoken numbers.

Scope: the Jan 2026 and Q3 FY26 decks and the Q1 and Q2 FY27 calls and decks. Calls of the merged business before Aug 2026 were not available, so this is the full ledger for the documents read, not the full record since the merger.

The pattern: the far-off revenue targets moved up (Rs 900 cr to Rs 1,000 cr; Rs 1,200 cr by FY29 to Rs 1,500 cr by FY30), while the near-term building blocks moved down (CDMO halved, registrations cut from 400+ to 333). Both missed items concern money raised from shareholders.

What this means for you

Use the near-term building blocks (CDMO revenue, registrations, Ibuprofen sales) as the real guidance, not the headline targets. Q3 FY27 settles rows 2, 3 and 12.

S12 | Guidance and outlook

The next six months carry the whole FY27 promise

Above 50% growth for FY27 needs second-half sales of Rs 533 cr, +63.5% on last year and +13.4% above the Q2 run rate; there is no formal FY28 guidance.

Next quarter, in their words: Ibuprofen "approximately Rs 20 crores" in Q3, and margin moving toward 22% [call p4]. No Q3 revenue number was given; we do not infer one.

What this means for you

S16 | The causal chain

Approvals and launches are proven; turning repeat orders into cash is not

S19 | Risks and anti-thesis

The case against is about cash and concentration, not about the margin

The strongest case against is that profit is not yet cash and growth rests on one partner molecule; the largest single risk is a second-half miss, worth about Rs 72 a share.

The anti-thesis, argued as a sceptic would

  • Profit has not become cash: operating cash flow Rs -260 cr over three years; inventory plus receivables up Rs 131.8 cr in a year, more than twice H1 profit of Rs 55.5 cr.
  • Growth is narrow: one partner molecule was Rs 83 cr of H1 sales; two molecules were 26% of FY26 sales; new customers gave 72-81% of the H1 increase.
  • Guidance keeps moving: 7 of 22 claims changed; CDMO for FY27 halved between Aug and Nov 2026.
  • About 32% of the market value is cash and a stake valued by management, carried at Rs 32 cr.
  • The cash is not obviously the minorities': Rs 319 cr in unquoted instruments, a loan without terms, and shares of the promoter entity that funds the company.
  • More dilution is coming even with net cash: a promoter warrant issue for a plan called self-funded.
  • The price already assumes delivery: at the bear FY27 EBITDA of Rs 156 cr, the core is 13.2x a number not yet shown in cash.

If the stock halves in two years, the most likely cause is all three together: H2 FY27 at the Q2 run rate, a fourth year of negative operating cash flow, and no Synthimed sale.
