## 0A. Why we are covering this business

Written from the documents in plain words, in under 15 lines.

- What it is: Concord Biotech grows microbes in 1,250 m3 of steel fermentation tanks in Gujarat to make complex drug ingredients (APIs), mainly for organ-transplant drugs, and sells them by the kilogram to generic drug makers in 70+ countries [deck p18, p14]. Since 2016 it also turns its own APIs into tablets, capsules and now injectables [deck p17].
- Why it caught our attention: after years of steady growth, FY26 revenue fell 12% to Rs 1,055 cr and EBITDA margin fell from 42% to 35% [Screener], yet H1 FY27 revenue is back up 25.6% to Rs 566.6 cr [deck p10].
- Why now: Q2 FY27 results (27 Oct 2026) showed API sales up 30% [deck p7]; management repeated 18-19%+ growth and a 38-40% margin path on the 28 Oct 2026 call [call p8, p12]; a 1:1 bonus was allotted on 4 Nov 2026 [BSE ann].
- The core debate: was FY26 a one-year pause by cautious customers that is now reversing, with margins back to 38-40% as Unit III (55% used), Unit II (21%) and Unit IV (5%) fill up [call p18], or has the business stepped down to slower growth and lower margins?
- What the market seems to believe: at Rs 594 the market value is Rs 12,418 cr, 44.5x trailing PAT of Rs 279 cr [Screener]; at 30x in FY29 the price needs PAT of about Rs 414 cr, a 14% yearly rise for three years [E], so the market already assumes a recovery.
- What would make us care, and what would make us drop it: we care if H2 FY27 revenue reaches about Rs 689 cr (the level 19% full-year growth needs, +14% on H2 FY26) with EBITDA margin above 36%; we drop it if Q3 FY27 API growth falls below 10% or Unit IV has no signed injectable contract by the Q4 FY27 call.
- What the reader will be able to decide: whether the recovery is real and priced, what value range the guided path supports against Rs 594, and which dated numbers to watch to know early if it is going wrong.

## 0B. How to think

Concord is a fermentation API maker with a newer, loss-making formulation and injectable arm attached.
Its value depends on two things: whether the core API business is back to its old growth after the FY26 fall, and whether the new plants built in FY21 to FY25 earn a return.
The job is NOT to retell the investor presentation; the job is to prove, with numbers from 14 calls, 14 decks and 3 annual reports, whether FY26 was a pause or a step down, and what the new capacity is really worth.

### Connecting the dots

Fixed assets rose from Rs 575 cr in FY24 to Rs 795 cr in FY25 as CWIP fell from Rs 211 cr to Rs 51 cr [Screener]: Unit IV (injectables) and the formulation expansion went into use.
Depreciation then rose from Rs 54 cr in FY25 to Rs 74 cr in FY26 [Screener], while Unit IV still runs at 5% utilisation [call p18].
Management says EBITDA margin excluding the injectables unit and Stellon would be 37.1% in Q2 FY27, against 33.8% reported [call p7, deck p6]: 3.3 points on Rs 309.1 cr is about Rs 10.2 cr of EBITDA in one quarter, and 4.8 points on H1 sales of Rs 566.6 cr is about Rs 27.2 cr in the half [E].
So roughly Rs 40-55 cr a year of EBITDA cost plus about Rs 20 cr of extra depreciation sits in units that earn almost nothing yet [E]; that is about a fifth of TTM PAT of Rs 279 cr before tax effects.
For the thesis this means the margin gap is mostly a fill-the-plant question, and the evidence to track is signed injectable contracts and Unit IV utilisation, not the API business.
