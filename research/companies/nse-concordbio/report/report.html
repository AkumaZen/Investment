Concord Biotech Ltd

A pause in volume, a step down in margin, and a price that expects a full recovery

Company study, not a recommendation  |  Data as of 9 November 2026  |  Rs crore unless stated  |  Per-share figures after the 1:1 bonus

S1 | Summary

A strong niche business on a pause, priced for a full recovery

Concord is a high-quality fermentation API maker; FY26 looks like a pause in buying, not lost customers, but the margin has not come back and the price already assumes it will.

Concord Biotech makes complex drug ingredients by fermentation, the way beer or yoghurt is made but in steel tanks under drug-plant rules. Few companies can do this well, which is why it earned 40%+ operating margins for years.

In FY26 sales fell 12% and profit 30%, the first fall since listing. This report answers one question first: was FY26 a pause or a step down? Our answer: a pause in volumes, but a step down in margin that has not yet reversed. Half-year sales are back up 26%, led by ingredient (API) sales up 36%. But margin is 33-34% against 42% two years ago, part of the recovery was won with lower prices, and two new businesses (injectables and a US sales arm) still lose money.

At Rs 594 the market is paying about 44.5 times the last year's profit. That price needs close to management's full plan: about 19% sales growth a year and margins back to 38% by FY29. For FY27 alone, 19% growth needs second-half sales of about Rs 689 cr, +14% on last year's second half.

Management has met 10 of 23 promises that have come due across 14 earnings calls. It is open about what went wrong but late on timing, so we rate it WATCH, not RED.

S2 | The business

A microbe, a tank and a regulatory file: why this niche pays well

Concord grows microbes in tanks to make hard-to-make drug ingredients, sells them by the kg to generic drug makers, and keeps customers because switching suppliers needs a new regulatory filing.

The revenue equation

Sales = tank capacity used × batches a year × kg per batch × price per kg, across 30+ molecules, plus finished medicines made from those APIs.

Every term matters. Capacity is fixed until a new plant is built. Yield per batch comes from years of process know-how. Price depends on how many other approved makers a buyer can switch to, usually two or three.

From microbe to medicine

Why this business is hard to copy

  • A DMF ties a customer in. A generic maker's approval names its API source. Switching means new testing and a filing change, which takes time and money [judgment], so suppliers are rarely dropped for small price gaps.
  • Fermentation is slow to start. A new plant needs strains, years of process tuning and large tanks; Limbasi alone cost about Rs 400 cr (Q1 FY24 call).
  • The market grows slowly; Concord grows by winning second-source slots. Management puts immunosuppressant market growth at about 10% a year and says its own growth comes from becoming a buyer's second supplier (Q2 FY27 call).

Where it sells

Glossary

What this means for you

This is a quality niche, not a commodity chemical maker. The risk is not that customers leave overnight; it is that price is used to win new slots, which lowers margins slowly. Watch gross margin each quarter.

The biggest threat: price, not a lost product

Management says FY26 was "not related to lower demand, loss of market share or pricing pressure" (Q4 FY26 call p4). But in Q1 FY27 it said share gains were "primarily driven by pricing" (Q1 FY27 call p10), and gross margin fell from 79.1% to 77.4% in H1 FY27. Competition is two or three makers per product, mostly European and Southeast Asian; management says China is not a major rival in its molecules (Q2 FY25 call p9). A second threat is quieter: emerging-market customers are buying the API and making their own tablets instead of buying Concord's tablets, which is why formulation sales fell 8% in H1 FY27.

Not disclosed: sales by molecule, the share of immunosuppressants in revenue, and the largest customer. We do not estimate them.

What this means for you

Do not pay for the new bets yet. They are worth something only when they show up as revenue lines in the decks. Oncology doubling is the most concrete new driver so far.

S4 | Segments

The API engine is back; formulations and the start-ups are holding margins down

API sales (83% of H1 FY27) are growing again, formulations are shrinking, and the two start-ups cost about 4 margin points; even without them the core margin is 37%, below the 38-40% guide.

The company reports one segment in its accounts, but the decks split sales into API and formulations. API fell 11.8% in FY26 and rebounded 36% in H1 FY27. Formulations fell 13.0% in FY26 and another 8% in H1 FY27.

Margin with and without the start-ups

Management now reports a second margin that leaves out the injectables plant and Stellon. In Q2 FY27 it was 37.1% against 33.8% reported; for H1 FY27, 37.8% against 33.0%; for FY26, 39% against 35% (Q2 FY27 call p5, p7; Q4 FY26 call p5). That gap is about Rs 42 cr of EBITDA in FY26 and Rs 27 cr in H1 FY27 [E]. But the adjusted margin itself fell from 41% in Q2 FY26 to 37.1% in Q2 FY27, so the core business is also earning less, not only the start-ups.

What this means for you

Judge FY27 on two numbers the company now gives every quarter: reported margin and the margin without start-ups. If both rise together, the recovery is real. If only the adjusted one rises, the start-ups are still bleeding.

S5 | Customers

No customer is big enough to hurt; the US hand-over is the open question

Customer risk is low and falling: the top 10 buy 38% of sales and none buys more than 10%; the open item is the hand-over of US formulation sales from Advergent to Stellon.

Who buys

  • 300+ customers in 70+ countries; no single customer is above 10% of sales in FY26 or FY25 (annual report p247).
  • The 50% Japan joint venture bought Rs 29.1 cr in FY26, 2.8% of sales, and is the only named related customer (annual report p244).
  • The largest customer is not named. Customer churn is not disclosed.

Who partners

  • Advergent: the US partner for Concord's ANDA products; the business is moving to Concord's own Stellon (Q2 FY27 call p4).
  • Stellon Biotech: 75% owned US arm; commercial operations began Q1 FY27; contribution now expected in H2 FY27, one half later than first said.
  • CDMO partner: one unnamed innovator; Concord is the exclusive supplier of its new drug, about USD 2 mn a year.

What this means for you

A lost customer is unlikely to sink the year. A slow Stellon start would, at the margin, keep US formulation sales weak; check that Stellon shows revenue in the H2 FY27 numbers.

S6 | Moat

The moat protects customers, not prices

Concord's moat is real: few rivals, sticky customers and top-tier margins; but it did not prevent a volume fall in FY26 or price cuts in FY27.

Three things protect Concord: 1,250 m3 of fermentation tanks that took 20 years to build, 150+ drug master files that tie customers in, and a cost position that still gives the best margin among Indian API makers except Divi's.

Competition, as management describes it

  • Two or three makers per product (Q3 FY26 call p14).
  • Only one other fermentation company competes on four or five of its molecules (Q1 FY25 call p11).
  • Rivals are mainly European and Southeast Asian; little from China (Q2 FY25 call p9).
  • Biocon is the listed Indian rival in immunosuppressants; it is not a clean comparison because most of its business is biosimilars.
  • Independent market-share data was not found; the share gain claims are management's.

S7 | Growth quality

Growth is back, but it is lower-quality growth than before

The H1 FY27 rebound is mostly volume and currency with some price cuts, so profit grew 19% on 26% sales growth; returns will recover only when Units III and IV fill up.

Growth is only worth paying for if it comes with good returns. Concord's growth from FY21 to FY25 did: return on capital stayed at 26-32% outside FY22. In FY26 return on capital fell to 17% and margin to 35%, because sales fell while the new plants added fixed costs.

Quality of the growth that is coming back: H1 FY27 sales rose 26%, EBITDA 25% and profit 19%. Profit grew slower than sales because gross margin fell (solvents, power and fuel, product mix, and price), and other income fell.

What this means for you

Look past headline sales growth. The number that tells you quality is back is gross margin returning towards 79-80%; it was 76.2% in Q2 FY27.

S8 | The FY26 fall

A pause in volume, a step down in margin

Volumes look like a pause: API sales are growing again and the trailing year (Rs 1,170 cr) is back above FY24; but margin and pricing have stepped down and have not yet recovered.

Start with what FY25 really was. Its fourth quarter (Rs 430 cr) was 35% above the year before, mostly in domestic API sales. In FY26, management said customers moved away from "bulk purchases" compared with the previous year (Q4 FY26 call p4). So FY25 included front-loaded buying, and FY26 paid for it. Taken together, FY24 to FY26 sales grew 1.9% a year.

S9 | Capacity

The tanks for FY29 already exist; the empty plants are the drag

There is enough fermentation capacity for the guided plan to FY29; the issue is two finished-dose plants running at 5% and 21%, which add costs before they add sales.

Concord has four plants in Gujarat. The two fermentation plants make almost all the API; the two Valthera plants make finished medicines and are mostly empty.

When is the next fermentation plant needed?

Management will add capacity at Limbasi once Unit III reaches 80-85%. At 18% API growth, Unit III goes from 55% to 80% in about 2.3 years [E], so a new block is a FY29-FY30 decision. If the analyst estimate of 20-25% without KSMs is right, it is later.

Absence test: no new fermentation capacity beyond 1,250 m3 has been announced. Management says Limbasi could earn Rs 1,500-1,600 cr at peak against about Rs 600 cr from Dholka (Q1 FY24 call), so the existing tanks can carry the plan to FY29.

What this means for you

This lowers one risk: Concord does not need a big new plant to grow, so cash will build. It raises another: depreciation is already up from Rs 54 cr to Rs 74 cr, and it only pays off if Unit IV fills.

S12 | Guidance

A 19% year needs a 14% better second half

For FY27 growth of 19%, H2 sales must reach about Rs 689 cr, up 14% on H2 FY26; margin must then climb about 4 points over three years to reach the bottom of the 38-40% guide.

Current guidance, from the Q2 FY27 call: FY27 growth "slightly better than our historical growth of 18%"; EBITDA margin 38-40% "over the next few years"; tax 24-25%; capex Rs 20-30 cr plus Rs 50-60 cr a year in FY27 and FY28.

What this means for you

The growth half looks reachable: H1 grew 26% and H2 only needs 14%. The margin half is the harder test, because it needs the start-ups to stop losing money and prices to hold.

S13 | Triggers

The next two results matter more than any new product

The strongest near-term triggers are H2 FY27 sales and gross margin; injectables and Stellon are moderate; CDMO, Inspira and new molecules are long-dated and small.

Absence test. Things a fast-growing Concord would have done by now but has not: announced new fermentation capacity beyond 1,250 m3; signed an injectable contract; shown Stellon revenue; given an adjusted margin above 40% since Q2 FY26. Each absence is consistent with a business that is recovering, not yet accelerating.

What this means for you

Mark mid-February 2027 (Q3 FY27) and end-May 2027 (FY27) in your diary. Those two releases settle most of the debate in this report.

S14 | Peers

Cheaper than API peers because it stopped growing

Concord has top-tier margins but trades at a discount to API and CDMO peers because it was the only one to shrink in FY26; the discount narrows only if growth returns.

Reading the peer table

  • Every listed API and CDMO peer grew 7-37% in FY26 while Concord fell 12%. That is why its multiple is lower.
  • Its margin is still second only to Divi's.
  • The API and CDMO group trades at 58-102x; Concord at 43.6x on Screener's basis (44.5x on our TTM calculation).
  • No listed company is a pure fermentation peer, so these are reference points, not a fair-value anchor.
  • Our base multiple of 35x on FY29 earnings sits below the whole API group for this reason.

What this means for you

The peer group says Concord is not expensive for its quality, but also that the market already gives it credit for a recovery: 44x is not a distressed multiple.

S15 | Reality vs options

Pay for the API engine; treat the rest as free options

About 83% of sales today is fermentation API; injectables, CDMO, Inspira and the Rs 3,000 cr target are options with no contracts or revenue lines yet.

Our base case uses guided growth of 19% a year and no more. It reaches Rs 1,778 cr of sales in FY29, about 59% of the Rs 3,000 cr target. If the options work, they are upside to the bull case; we do not add a separate value for them, because none yet has a contract, a revenue line or a disclosed margin.

What this means for you

If you buy into the story because of injectables or CDMO, you are paying for things that do not show in the numbers yet. Our values do not depend on them.

S16 | Cause and effect

Two chains decide the margin; each has one weak link

Margin recovers through two chains: injectable contracts removing a Rs 40 cr-a-year drag, and API volumes filling Limbasi; the first needs a signature, the second needs prices to hold.

The weak link in chain 1 is the first box: a contract. The weak link in chain 2 is the third box: if volumes come back at lower prices, the margin stays near 37% even with full tanks.

What this means for you

Watch the first box of each chain, not the last. A signed injectable contract and a stable gross margin will show up before the margin does.

S19 | Risks

Most risks show up in two numbers: H2 sales and gross margin

The strongest case against Concord is that FY25 was a peak, prices are softening and the new businesses keep slipping; four of eight risks feed directly into our bear case.

If the share halves

At Rs 297 the market would value Concord at about 22.3 times trailing profit, near Aarti Drugs' 19.6x, a commodity API maker. That would mean the market had decided FY26 was a step down: growth near 10% and margins stuck at 33-34%. Our bear case (Rs 352) is built on exactly that and sits above Rs 297, because the cash pile and existing tanks still earn something. The share has already traded at Rs 494 in the last year.
