## 1. Why it matters and the core debate

Concord is one of a handful of companies worldwide that make immunosuppressant APIs by fermentation (Tacrolimus, Mycophenolate, Cyclosporine, Sirolimus) [deck p16].
After nine years of growth, FY26 revenue fell 12% to Rs 1,055 cr and EBITDA margin dropped from 42% to 35% [Screener].
H1 FY27 revenue is back up 25.6% to Rs 566.6 cr [deck p10], but margin is 33.0%, still far below the 38-40% management calls normal [call p12].
Core debate: is FY26 a one-year customer pause that is now reversing, with margins returning to 38-40% as Unit III (55%), Unit II (21%) and Unit IV (5%) fill up [call p18], or has the business stepped down to a lower-growth, lower-margin level that a 44x trailing P/E does not price?

## 2. How one unit earns money

Fermentation: microbes grow in large steel tanks (1,250 m3 in total) and produce a complex molecule, which is purified into an API (the active ingredient of a medicine) and sold by the kg to generic drug makers worldwide [deck p18, p19].
Revenue = kg sold x price per kg, across 30+ APIs; the moat is the strain, the yield and the regulatory filings (150+ DMFs) that a customer has to re-validate before switching [deck p14, p20].
Formulations (since FY2016): Concord turns its own APIs into tablets, capsules and now injectables, sold as branded generics in India, to emerging-market partners and through ANDAs in the US (12 approved, 7 marketed) [deck p17, p20].
Product lines: immunosuppressants, oncology, anti-bacterials, anti-fungals, statins and others; CDMO (one commercial project, about USD 2 mn a year) [call p13]; injectables (Unit IV) [deck p19]; the Inspira dermatology division (launched Q2 FY27) [call p11].
Split: API Rs 250.2 cr (81%), formulation Rs 58.9 cr (19%) in Q2 FY27 [deck p7]; domestic Rs 178.7 cr, export Rs 130.4 cr [deck p8].

## 5. Anti-thesis (written first)

Revenue was Rs 1,017 cr in FY24 and Rs 1,055 cr in FY26: two years of 1.9% CAGR [Screener].
The FY26 margin fall from 42% to 35% came with a jump in fixed costs: depreciation rose from 54 to 74 [Screener] after Unit IV was capitalised, and it is running at 5% utilisation [call p18].
Inventory days are 485 and the cash conversion cycle is 493 days [Screener]: each rupee of growth ties up more working capital than almost any peer.
The stock trades at 44.5x TTM PAT of Rs 279 cr [Screener]; at 30x in FY29 the price needs PAT of Rs 414 cr, a 14% CAGR, while the last two years show PAT falling 25% from Rs 372 cr [Screener].
Formulations shrank 8% in H1 [deck p7] because emerging-market customers are switching to buying the API instead, which erodes the forward integration story.
Q4 FY25 revenue was Rs 430 cr, against Rs 244 cr the quarter before [Screener]; whether that quarter held a one-off order or a pull-forward is unknown and must be checked in the Q4 FY25 call, because it sets the FY25 base that makes FY26 look weak.

## 9. Connecting the dots

Unit IV (injectables) and the formulation expansion were capitalised in FY25: fixed assets rose from Rs 575 cr to Rs 795 cr and CWIP fell from Rs 211 cr to Rs 51 cr [Screener].
Depreciation jumped from Rs 54 cr in FY25 to Rs 74 cr in FY26 [Screener].
Unit IV runs at 5% utilisation [call p18], and management says EBITDA margin excluding the injectables unit and Stellon would be 37.1% in Q2, against 33.8% reported [call p7].
3.3 points on Rs 309.1 cr is about Rs 10.2 cr of EBITDA a quarter, and 4.8 points on H1 sales of Rs 566.6 cr is about Rs 27.2 cr for the half [E].
So roughly Rs 40-55 cr a year of EBITDA plus Rs 20 cr of extra depreciation sits in two businesses that earn almost nothing yet; if they fill, PAT gains are large; if they do not, the drag is permanent.
