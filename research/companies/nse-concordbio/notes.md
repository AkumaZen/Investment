# Concord Biotech - answers to the 15 questions

Analysis date 9 Nov 2026; price Rs 594 post 1:1 bonus; 20,92,32,408 shares.
Each answer is tagged Disclosed (company document says it), Inferred (my reading of disclosed numbers, marked [E]) or Not disclosed.
Sources: evidence/guidance-ledger.md (ledger), evidence/deck-history.md (decks), evidence/ar-fy26.md (AR), data/screener-2026-11-09.md (Screener), evidence/peers.md, and the transcripts cited by page.

## Q1. Why did FY26 revenue fall 12%, what fell, and was it called in advance?

Disclosed.
Management gave five reasons on the Q4 FY26 call (p3-p4): US customers slowed buying in H1 on tariff uncertainty; a CDSCO written-confirmation delay stopped EU supplies for about 3 months; a major Middle East tender went into abeyance and other supplies to that region were constrained; customers moved from bulk to staggered quarterly buying in H2; and the US Veterans Affairs tender was not finalised.
It says the fall was "not related to lower demand, loss of market share or pricing pressure" (Q4 FY26 p4).
By segment, API fell 11.8% and formulation 13.0%; by market, India -14.8%, US -21.7%, other overseas -4.3% (AR p247).
Management explains the large India fall by Middle East formulation supplies being routed through Indian partners, so they book as domestic (Q4 FY26 p15).
Molecule-level detail is Not disclosed.
Called in advance: no; on the Q4 FY25 call (May 2025) management gave no yearly guide but said capacities and portfolio are designed for 25% growth (ledger G6, Q4 FY25 p17); the fall was explained after the fact.

## Q2. Was Q4 FY25 (Rs 430 cr) a one-off or a pull-forward?

Inferred.
Q4 FY25 was 35% above Q4 FY24, concentrated in domestic (+50%) and API (+37%); Q4 FY26 fell back to Rs 326 cr, almost exactly Q4 FY24's Rs 319 cr (decks).
The Q4 FY25 call did not call it a one-off; it said API sales "fluctuate" with "uneven customer procurement patterns" (Q4 FY25 p5).
The Q4 FY26 call says customers moved away from "bulk purchases" in H2 FY26 "compared to previous year" (p4), which is the company's own description of the FY25 pattern.
Reading [E]: Q4 FY25 was a bulk-buying quarter rather than a new run-rate, so FY25 (Rs 1,200 cr) overstates the base; FY24-FY26 together grew 1.9% a year, and FY27 TTM (Rs 1,170 cr) is the better base.

## Q3. How much of the API growth is volume, price and new customers?

Partly disclosed.
FY26: "no price growth"; all movement was volume (Q4 FY26 p8).
Q1 FY27: wallet-share gains were "primarily driven by pricing", that is, Concord offered competitive prices using its scale (Q1 FY27 p10); currency added about 10% to reported growth, about 3-5% net of input costs (Q1 FY27).
Q2 FY27: management said competitors are "significantly priced higher" (Q2 FY27 p10).
A split of the H1 FY27 API growth of 35.7% into volume, price and new customers is Not disclosed.
Reading [E]: the H1 recovery is mainly volume plus currency, bought partly with lower prices; this is a margin risk, and it matches the H1 gross margin contraction (Q2 FY27 p5).

## Q4. Who else makes these molecules, and is Concord gaining share?

Partly disclosed.
Management says there are 2-3 players per product (Q3 FY26 p14), "just one other company" in fermentation competes on about 4-5 molecules (Q1 FY25 p11), competition is mainly European and Southeast Asian with little from China (Q2 FY25 p9), and a Chinese Tacrolimus ER approval is not a concern (Q3 FY24 p12).
Competitors are not named by the company; Biocon is the listed fermentation rival (peers.md); Teva and Chinese makers are Not disclosed as competitors in company documents.
Share: management claims wallet-share gains in Q1 FY27 (p9-p10); independent market-share data is Not disclosed and was not found.

## Q5. How concentrated are sales, and who is the largest customer?

Disclosed.
Top-10 share fell from 47.7% (FY22) to 37.6% (FY26) (deck-q2fy27 p23); no customer is above 10% of revenue in FY26 or FY25 (AR p247).
The one named related customer is the 50% Japan JV, Rs 29.1 cr of sales (2.8%) (AR p244).
The largest customer's name is Not disclosed.

## Q6. What does each plant earn, and when is the next fermentation plant needed?

Partly disclosed.
Utilisation at Q2 FY27: Unit I 75%, Unit III 55%, Unit II 21%, Unit IV 5% (Q2 FY27 p18).
Revenue by plant is Not disclosed; management's Q1 FY24 rule of thumb was 450 m3 at Unit I yields about Rs 600 cr, so 800 m3 at Unit III could yield Rs 1,500-1,600 cr at a 75-80% peak (ledger, Q1 FY24 p11).
Management will add API capacity at Unit III once it reaches 80-85%, and only 20-25% of the 160-acre site is used (Q1 FY27 p13).
Unit III's 55% includes key starting material production; an analyst put it at 20-25% excluding KSMs and management did not reject it (Q1 FY27 p15).
Reading [E]: at 18% API growth, Unit III would reach 80% in roughly two to three years, so a new fermentation block is a FY29-FY30 decision; on the KSM-adjusted figure it is later.

## Q7. What do injectables plus Stellon cost, and when do they break even?

Disclosed cost, breakeven Not disclosed.
EBITDA margin excluding the injectables unit and Stellon: 39% vs 35% reported for FY26 (Q4 FY26 p5); 37.8% vs 33.0% for H1 FY27 and 37.1% vs 33.8% for Q2 FY27 (Q2 FY27 p5, p7).
Reading [E]: that is about Rs 42 cr of EBITDA in FY26 (4 points on Rs 1,055 cr) and about Rs 27 cr in H1 FY27 (4.8 points on Rs 566.6 cr), before the extra depreciation from Unit IV (Screener depreciation up from Rs 54 cr to Rs 74 cr).
The core business margin also fell: the adjusted margin was 41% in Q2 FY26 and 37.1% in Q2 FY27 (ledger), so not all of the gap to 38-40% is start-up drag.
Stellon alone lost Rs 7.2 cr at PAT in FY26 with nil turnover (AR p56).
Management declined to give a breakeven date (Q4 FY26 p6); injectable contracts are "expected to finalize in the near term" (Q2 FY27 p4).

## Q8. Why did formulations fall 8% in H1 FY27, and how big is the API switch?

Disclosed reason, size Not disclosed.
Two reasons: emerging-market customers increasingly buy the API instead of finished formulations, and the US business moved from partner Advergent to Stellon (Q2 FY27 p4); management said in Q3 FY26 that it takes emerging-market opportunities through formulation only where it cannot win them through API (Q3 FY26 p10).
Formulation revenue: Rs 105.5 cr in H1 FY26 to Rs 97.5 cr in H1 FY27 (decks).
Reading [E]: the switch is revenue-neutral or positive for the group if the API is still Concord's, but it weakens the forward-integration growth story that drove FY24-FY25.

## Q9. What did the FY21-FY23 capex build, and what has it earned?

Partly disclosed.
Net fixed assets plus CWIP rose from Rs 381 cr (FY20) to Rs 766 cr (FY23) and Rs 868 cr (FY26) (Screener).
What it built: Unit III Limbasi (800 m3, FY2021, about Rs 400 cr), the Unit II expansion to 869 mn units and softgel, and Unit IV injectables (FY2025) (decks, ledger).
Fixed asset turnover (sales / fixed assets, Screener) [E]: 1.77 in FY24, 1.51 in FY25, 1.34 in FY26.
Reading [E]: Unit III has paid back through FY22-FY25 growth; Unit IV has earned almost nothing so far (5% utilisation); the project-wise return is Not disclosed.

## Q10. Why did inventory days rise from 286 to 485?

Disclosed reason, Inferred context.
Management: goods made for Q4 shipment were held as customers staggered their buying, some shipments slipped in late March, and higher utilisation with long fermentation cycles adds work-in-progress (Q4 FY26 p15).
Inventory rose from about Rs 240 cr to Rs 326 cr (analyst figure on the call, Q4 FY26 p15).
Reading [E]: 485 days is not abnormal for Concord; Screener shows 503, 434 and 430 days in FY21-FY23; FY25's 286 was the outlier, flattered by the large Q4 FY25 shipments.
Watch item: inventory has to fall in H2 FY27 for the staggered-buying explanation to hold; H1 FY27 inventory is in the Sep-26 balance sheet but a half-year days figure is not computed here.

## Q11. How often has management hit its guidance?

Disclosed in the calls; scored in the ledger.
Across 14 calls, 23 promises fell due: 10 met and 13 missed or late (ledger, scorecard).
The 18-19% growth guide was missed in FY26 (-12%) and is now restated for FY27 as "slightly better than our historical growth of 18%" (Q2 FY27 p15); the 38-40% margin guide was missed in FY26 (35%) and H1 FY27 (33%).
Verdict AMBER: honest about facts and reasons, unreliable on timing and the size of medium-term targets (ledger).

## Q12. What is in the investment book, and is any with related parties?

Disclosed.
At Mar-26: mutual funds Rs 402.1 cr, Palvella Therapeutics (listed US stock, through OCI) Rs 84.1 cr, Clean Max Everglades (26% captive power associate) Rs 0.8 cr, cash Rs 12.2 cr, no borrowings (AR p238).
At Sep-26: cash Rs 7.7 cr plus current investments Rs 438.1 cr, about Rs 445 cr, and non-current investments Rs 96.0 cr (deck-q2fy27 p11).
Related parties: only Clean Max (immaterial); the Rs 14.6 cr loan to the 75% subsidiary Stellon, which the AR lists as a subsidiary "in which directors are interested" (AR p79), is the item to watch.
Who owns the other 25% of Stellon is Not disclosed in the pages read.

## Q13. Who are the promoters, who sold, and are shares pledged?

Main holders at Mar-26 (AR p160-p161): Sudhir Vaid 28.84%, Manju Vaid 9.55%, Sudman Consultants LLP 4.54%, Ankur Vaid 0.56%, Megha Vaid 0.52%, Sonal Kumra 0.07%.

Pledge: Screener's table shows no pledge column, and the AR does not mention one; the exchange shareholding filing was not checked, so pledge is Not verified.

## Q14. What are the US regulatory records of the plants?

Disclosed by the company; USFDA database Not checked.
- Unit III Limbasi: USFDA inspection June 2023, zero 483 observations, EIR received (Q1 FY24 p7, p10; Q4 FY24 p3); ANVISA approval in Q2 FY27 (Q2 FY27 p4).
- Unit I Dholka: USFDA inspection reported on the Q4 FY25 call "with 483 observations", all procedural and none on data integrity (Q4 FY25 p4); EIR received by Q2 FY26, meaning no regulatory action required (deck-q2fy26 p5).
- Unit II Valthera: USFDA inspected in March 2023 (Q1 FY24 p10); inspected again in Q2 FY27 with "one minor procedural observation unrelated to GMP" (deck-q2fy27 p5).
- Unit IV injectables: WHO-GMP certified (Q4 FY26 p4); no USFDA inspection disclosed.
- No warning letter or import alert is mentioned in any document read.
What is at risk [E]: Unit I and Unit III make all the fermentation API (about 80% of revenue); an adverse US outcome at either would hit US-linked API sales directly and customers' dossiers that name the site.

## Q15. What does Rs 594 imply for FY29 PAT, and how does it compare with guidance?

Inferred; the full model is model/valuation.py.
Market cap Rs 12,418 cr; at 30x FY29 earnings the price needs FY29 PAT of about Rs 414 cr, 14% a year for three years from TTM PAT of Rs 279 cr, or 16.9% a year from FY26 PAT of Rs 259 cr.
Guided path [E] (model/valuation.md, base case): 19% growth from FY26's Rs 1,055 cr gives Rs 1,778 cr in FY29; at 38% EBITDA margin and 24.5% tax, PAT is Rs 484 cr, above the Rs 411-414 cr the price needs at 30x if it simply holds.
If the investor also wants a 12% annual return to Mar-28, the price needs FY29 PAT of Rs 482 cr at 30x, almost exactly the guided Rs 484 cr, or Rs 413 cr at 35x.
So the price assumes management delivers close to its full guide; FY24-FY26 delivery (1.9% a year) is well short of it.
