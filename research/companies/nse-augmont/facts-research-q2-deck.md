## Critical scope and reconciliation facts

[Q2 deck p.26 physical] AEL standalone/core FY26 PAT is INR153 crore; H1 FY27 standalone PAT INR107 crore, +75% YoY, about 70% of FY26; Q2 standalone PAT about 2.5x YoY and 3.9x QoQ.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] AEL standalone Q2 FY27 EBITDA INR113 crore versus INR50 crore Q2 FY26; standalone PAT margin 0.65% versus 0.18% Q2 FY26.
[Q2 deck p.31 physical] Consolidated FY26 PAT INR348 crore is the deck's rounded group PAT; this deck does NOT identify profit attributable to parent owners separately and cannot substantiate an owner-profit INR347 crore claim.
[Q2 deck p.30 physical] Non-controlling interest is INR26 crore Mar26 and INR31 crore Sep26; this is equity, NOT a minority share of PAT.
[Q2 deck p.31 physical] Fresh issue is stated as INR620 crore, not INR625 crore; no reconciliation to another proceeds figure in this deck.
[Q2 deck p.7 physical] Registered consumers at Sep26 are 52.3 million versus 50.6 million Jun26; registered is NOT active customers.
[Q2 deck p.14 physical] 54+ million is Gold For All FY26 transaction count, versus 28+ million FY24, NOT registered consumers.
[Q2 deck p.14 physical] Gold For All revenue FY24 INR1,178 crore to FY26 INR6,687 crore; stated multiple 5.7x. Rounded 1,100 to 6,600 would arithmetically be 6x but is not the deck's exact series.
[Q2 deck p.14 physical] Digital gold revenue FY24 INR288 crore to FY26 INR2,159 crore, stated multiple 7.5x.
[Q2 deck p.10 physical] FY26 enterprise/international/SPOT platform revenue share 92.9%; consumer Gold For All 7.1%; enterprise includes refinery sales, ETF supply and exports, not only SPOT.
[Q2 deck p.27 physical] Q2 FY27 enterprise revenue INR13,852 crore (-32% YoY); consumer revenue INR1,984 crore (+47% YoY).
[Q2 deck p.13 physical] Scrap share of TOTAL PROCUREMENT ~3% Q2 FY26 to ~24.5% Q2 FY27 (~8x); deck does not specify whether denominator is procurement value or weight.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Management: scrap higher margin but 3?4 day working-capital cycle versus regular flows 1.8?2 days; fewer turns lower revenue; scrap margin benefit expected structural.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Management: silver margin benefit arises from import restrictions, DGFT licence and imports via IIBX; situational, not sustainable run rate.
[Q2 deck p.28 physical] Management: SPOT working-capital intensive; higher gold/silver prices cause same capital to buy fewer tonnes, reducing volumes.
[Q2 deck p.15 physical] Management claims bank debt cannot fund gold purchases; growth requires equity-funded working capital.
[Q2 deck p.10 physical] Two refineries at Rudrapur and Mumbai, combined 284 TPA; deck gives no metal split, utilisation or actual refinery output. Do NOT compare combined capacity to enterprise trading tonnes as utilisation.
[Q2 deck p.30 physical] Cash INR171 crore and bank balances other than cash INR50 crore Sep26; deck gives no restrictions/earmarking detail, so INR221 crore cannot automatically be called freely available cash.
[Q2 deck p.30 physical] Sep26 borrowings INR3 crore total (1 non-current + 2 current), leases INR7 crore total (5+2); analyst arithmetic net cash excl leases and excluding other bank balances INR168 crore (171?3), not a disclosed net-debt metric.
[Q2 deck p.30 physical] Equity share capital INR42 crore Mar26 to INR46 crore Sep26; no face value or diluted share count in deck, so share count cannot be inferred safely here.

[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Management Commentary
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Q2 FY27 was a strong quarter for Augmont, with record profitability and meaningful margin expansion despite lower revenues. The
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] performance reflects an improving business mix and our continued focus on quality of earnings and sustainable profitability. At its core,
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Augmont is a technology-first precious metals company, and this quarter showed how that platform model converts scale and ecosystem
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] depth into earnings.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Consolidated PAT rose to ₹120 Cr, the highest in Augmont’s history, up 98% QoQ and 7% YoY, with PAT margin at 0.76%, compared with
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] 0.32% in Q1 FY27 and 0.52% in Q2 FY26. EBITDA grew 16% YoY to ₹181 Cr, while EBITDA margin improved to 1.14% from 0.72% a year ago and
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] 0.44% in the previous quarter.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] This was achieved on lower revenue, which reflecting a deliberate choice. Revenue from operations was ₹15,836 Cr, down 27.0% YoY and
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] 16.4% QoQ, with softer demand and government measures on gold and silver flows also playing a part. In line with the national priority of
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] reducing gold imports, we scaled up domestic scrap procurement from about 3% of total procurement in Q2 FY26 to roughly 24.5%, an
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] increase of about 8x. Scrap earns higher margins but ties up more capital. Its working capital cycle is 3-4 days, compared to 1.8-2 days for
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] regular flows, resulting in fewer capital turns and lower reported revenue. We accepted that trade-off for better quality of earnings and a
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] stronger sourcing ecosystem. The six-month performance provides a more balanced perspective. H1 revenue was only 4.0% below H1 FY26,
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] while H1 EBITDA rose 2% to ₹265 Cr. H1 PAT of ₹181 Cr remained broadly stable despite moderation in international sales moderated YoY.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] The benefit of this approach is clearest in our core business. Augmont Enterprises Limited (AEL) standalone Q2 FY27 PAT was ₹85 Cr, about
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] 2.5x Q2 FY26 and 3.9x Q1 FY27, with PAT margin at 0.65% compared with 0.18% in Q2FY26, even as revenue was lower. EBITDA rose to ₹113 Cr
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] from ₹50 Cr a year ago. This is not a one-quarter effect. AEL’s H1 PAT of ₹107 Cr is 75% higher than H1 FY26 and already about 70% of its full-
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] year FY26 PAT of ₹153 Cr. Two factors drove the improvement. Higher-margin scrap is structural, and we expect it to carry forward. Silver
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] was the second. When silver became a restricted commodity, the strength of the Augmont ecosystem and our multi-source procurement
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] strategy came into play. We secured the DGFT licence and imported via IIBX while others faced supply challenges. As this benefit is linked
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] to current import restrictions, we view it as situational rather than a sustainable run-rate benefit.
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] Together, these results show a platform that is growing in quality as well as scale. Augmont now serves 52.3+ million registered consumers
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] and is India’s No. 1 Gold Platform. With our agreement with NSE for Electronic Gold Receipts (EGR), Augmont is well placed to lead the
[Q2 deck p.26 physical] formalisation of India’s gold ecosystem.
